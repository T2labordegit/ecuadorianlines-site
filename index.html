    What we carry

    If it fits the hold,
    it travels.

    Each Island-class ship combines refrigerated holds for palletized fruit, deck space for reefer and dry containers, and its own cranes. That mix lets us take cargo container ships turn away.

    Cold chain below deck

    Insulated, temperature-controlled holds carry palletized fruit and frozen cargo, with reefer plugs for containers on deck.

    Works any berth

    Ship's cranes load and discharge at ports and terminals without container gantries, including smaller Caribbean and Pacific ports.

    Direct to the market

    A dedicated fruit service into Vlissingen and Hamburg, close to the distribution centers of Northern Europe.

    Ice class, all five

    Every Island-class ship is ice-strengthened, so fruit keeps moving to North Sea and Baltic ports through the winter, when ice closes them to ordinary ships.

    Heritage

    Five decades
    on the reefer trade

    Ecuadorian Line goes back to Luis Noboa Naranjo (1916–1994). He started exporting Ecuadorian bananas in the late 1940s, and in the 1970s he began carrying them in his own ships. Today Ecuadorian Line is a proud member of the Noboa Corporation.

    1960

    The Antwerp connection

    From 1960 the Antwerp importer Léon Van Parys takes Noboa fruit every week, opening the door to Northern Europe. The relationship grows into ownership, and Antwerp becomes the group's European base.

    1975

    The first ship: Bonita

    The founder buys his first refrigerated ship, the Bering Core, and renames it Bonita. She was later lost in the English Channel.

    1979

    Ecuador's own reefer line

    He announces Ecuador's first private refrigerated shipping line. In January 1979 the Provincia de El Oro, his first Ecuadorian-flag ship, opens the Guayaquil–New York route.

    1989

    Building new ships

    The Atlantic Ocean is launched at the Uljanik shipyard in Pula as the fleet keeps growing. By 1994 the group runs 18 ships.

    1993

    The Island class

    Ahead of new European import rules he orders five purpose-built reefers from Danyard in Denmark: Albemarle, Barrington, Charles, Duncan and Hood Island. The first, Albemarle Island, is launched in 1993.

    Today

    Still on the run

    All five Islands are still in service on the reefer trade between the west coast of South America and Northern Europe.
